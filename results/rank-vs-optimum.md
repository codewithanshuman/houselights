# Optimizer vs booking the top-k by Qloo rank

Generated by `node scripts/evaluate-rank-vs-optimum.mjs` (seed 11, 400 scenarios per brief, no provider calls). Each scenario perturbs fees (×0.80–1.25), makes each act unavailable with probability 0.10, and sets the budget at 70–150% of the top-k cost. Only the 9 briefs with a complete Qloo ranking and affinity scores are used.

**Finding 1: re-ranking is rare and comes from near-ties.** Where the top-k acts by Qloo rank are bookable, the optimizer chooses differently in 177 of 1517 scenarios (11.7%) under affinity fit, against 0 (0.0%) under ordinal fit. Every difference comes from briefs where Qloo's scores are nearly tied: New York. In New York, Sault (rank 2, affinity 0.9580) and Jordan Rakei (rank 3, 0.9579) are 0.0001 apart. Margin-aware fit lets Rakei's local and audience-comparison evidence and lower fee decide. Ordinal fit treats rank 2 vs 3 as a 0.5 fit gap. Elsewhere fit dominates the declared policy, and the optimizer books Qloo's top-k.

**Finding 2: the main contribution is constraint handling.**

In 2083 of 3600 scenarios (57.9%) the top-k by rank could not be booked under the budget, fees, availability or genre rules:
- in 1380 of them the optimizer found the best bookable lineup;
- in the other 703 it reported that no lineup satisfies the constraints instead of inventing one.

The pitch is therefore *constraint-correct, auditable booking decisions with Qloo receipts*, not "better than Qloo's ranking". This result does not measure ticket sales or decision quality. Fees and availability are illustrative.
